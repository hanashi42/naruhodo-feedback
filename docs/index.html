Naruhodo — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 13, 2026

Overview

Naruhodo is a Chrome extension that helps you read Japanese by providing furigana and word lookups. We are committed to protecting your privacy. This policy describes what data the extension processes and how it is handled. In short: Naruhodo operates no servers, and the text of the pages you read is never sent anywhere while you read. Only two things ever go over the internet, and each only when you press it: pressing play on a word fetches that word's pronunciation recording from an outside audio service (it can be switched off — see “Audio Pronunciation”), and pressing Send on a card's Report sends that report, which you see in full first, to the developer — see “Reporting a problem with a card”. If you choose to use Send to Anki, your saved words also go to the Anki app on your own computer, and nowhere else — see “Send to Anki”.

Data Stored in Your Browser

The following data is stored in your browser and is never sent to Naruhodo or any third-party service. The one exception is your own choice: words you send to Anki are handed to the Anki app on your computer (see “Send to Anki”).

  • Vocabulary bank — Words you save, including the word, reading, definition, source sentence, page URL, page title, tags, and save timestamp. Stored in chrome.storage.local. It is never written to Chrome's sync area and never sent anywhere, except to your own Anki app when you use Send to Anki. One local copy does leave that storage area: once you have saved a handful of words, the extension writes an automatic backup — a plain-text JSON file containing the whole vocabulary bank, including source sentences and page URLs — to your Downloads folder at most once a week, plus one copy right after the extension updates to a new version. Those files stay on your disk until you delete them, including after you uninstall the extension.
  • Known words — The list of dictionary word IDs you mark as "known". Stored in chrome.storage.local, and also written to Chrome's sync area (see "Chrome Sync" below).
  • Extension settings — Your preferences such as hover mode, furigana mode, and theme. Stored in chrome.storage.local, and also written to Chrome's sync area (see "Chrome Sync" below).
  • Dictionary data — The bundled dictionary (295,000+ entries from JMdict and JMnedict, plus JLPT, pitch-accent, and example-sentence data) loaded into IndexedDB on first install. This data is shipped with the extension, not downloaded from the internet.
  • Pronunciation clips — Recordings you have played are kept in their own IndexedDB database so the same word is not fetched twice. It holds a few hundred clips and drops the least recently used beyond that; uninstalling the extension removes it.
  • Page content analysis — When you visit a Japanese web page, the extension reads text on the page to provide furigana and hover lookups. This text is processed entirely within your browser and never leaves it. Two things can leave, each only when you press it: the single dictionary word you press play on (“Audio Pronunciation” below), and a report you choose to send from a card, which can include the sentence and what the page printed over the word (“Reporting a problem with a card” below).

Chrome Sync

Your known-words list and extension settings are written to chrome.storage.sync so they can follow you across your own devices and survive a reinstall. If you have Chrome Sync enabled in your browser, Chrome transmits this data through your own Google account — the same mechanism Chrome uses to sync bookmarks. Naruhodo has no access to this transmission and no servers of its own. If Chrome Sync is disabled, this data never leaves your device. Your vocabulary bank is never written to the sync area.

Data Sent to External Services

Naruhodo has no servers, so nothing is ever sent to us. All core processing — dictionary lookups, tokenization, furigana generation, grammar annotation — happens entirely within your browser.

One feature makes a network request of its own: audio pronunciation, described in the next section. It sends one dictionary word to an outside audio service, and only when you press play.

Two features hand work to services built into your browser:

  • Selection translation — Selection translation uses Chrome's built-in translator: your text never leaves the browser, but Chrome itself may download a language pack the first time you translate into a language (English, or the language you choose in Naruhodo's settings).
  • The device voice — when pronunciation uses your browser's own speech synthesis, some voices are synthesized on your device and some are synthesized online by your browser vendor; when a voice is an online one, your browser sends the text being spoken to that vendor. Naruhodo always prefers an on-device Japanese voice when your system has one, and if your system has no Japanese voice at all nothing is spoken rather than a voice that would mispronounce the word.

Send to Anki

If you use Send to Anki (in the vocabulary bank, or + Anki on a word card once you have connected), Naruhodo sends the saved word's fields — the word, reading, meaning, JLPT level, pitch accent, the sentence you saved it with, the page title and address, and your tags — to the Anki app on your own computer, through the AnkiConnect add-on at 127.0.0.1. This never leaves your computer and never reaches Naruhodo or anyone else. Until you press Send to Anki once, Naruhodo does not contact Anki at all. Once you have connected, opening the card of a saved word, or the vocabulary bank, also asks your Anki whether that word is already there and which decks you have, so the button can show it; “Hide + Anki on word cards” in the vocabulary bank stops the cards from asking. What Anki then does with your cards (for example, syncing them through your AnkiWeb account) is governed by Anki.

Reporting a problem with a card

The Report button on a word card opens a small panel on the card. It shows the report exactly as it will be sent: the word, the reading and meanings the card showed, its level, the sentence the word was looked up in, the Naruhodo version and your browser (for example “Chrome 155 · macOS”). You can add a note about what is wrong, and untick “Include the sentence” to leave the sentence out. Nothing is sent until you press Send; closing the panel sends nothing. The page address is included only if you tick “Include the page address” (unticked by default), and then only the site and the path — never the part after “?” or “#”, where sites keep search terms and session codes. When you look up a selection the dictionary has no entry for, that card has the same Report: its report names the text you selected, the readings printed over it on the page, and the sentence it is in (again untickable), in place of what a word card showed. So do the cards for a particle, a grammar pattern, a conjugation and a katakana word the dictionary does not have: their report quotes the text the card showed, and the sentence (again untickable). Any card’s report also names what is printed on the page over the characters you pointed at — a reading or an English gloss, Naruhodo’s or the page’s own — when there is one.

When you press Send, the report is posted to a Google Form owned by the developer, without your Google sign-in or cookies; it contains no name, email address or account. As with any web request, Google sees standard metadata such as your IP address, under Google's privacy policy. Reports are read by the developer only to fix Naruhodo, are not published, and are never sold or shared. To keep the form from being flooded, Naruhodo stores the times of your last reports on your computer and allows a few per hour.

Audio Pronunciation

When you press play on a word — on a hover card, or in your vocabulary bank — with “Native recordings” enabled (the default), Naruhodo requests that word's pronunciation clip from the JapanesePod101 dictionary audio service (assets.languagepod101.com, which redirects the download to that vendor's own CDN, currently cdn.innovativelanguage.com). The request contains the dictionary word and its kana reading — nothing else. As with any web request, standard metadata such as your IP address is visible to that service. Clips are cached on your device, so playing the same word again reaches no further than that cache; it holds a few hundred clips, and one that ages out is fetched again the next time you play that word. Words shown in a conjugated form, and name entries, are spoken by your device's voice and never trigger a request. Nothing is requested while you read or hover — only when you press play. Choose “Device voice only” in the toolbar and Naruhodo makes no pronunciation requests at all — pronunciation then uses your browser’s own speech engine. One caveat that is the browser’s, not Naruhodo’s: if the only Japanese voice your browser offers is an online one, the browser itself sends the spoken text to its voice vendor.

Data We Do Not Collect

  • We do not collect personal information (name, email, etc.).
  • We do not collect browsing history or page URLs (except what you explicitly save to your local vocabulary bank, which stays on your device, or choose to include in a report you send).
  • We do not use analytics, tracking pixels, or any third-party tracking services.
  • We do not sell or share your data, and we transfer nothing to a third party for advertising, profiling, or any purpose beyond the audio request and the reports you choose to send, both described above.

Chrome Web Store User Data Policy

Naruhodo's use and transfer of user data adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Permissions

The extension requests the following browser permissions:

  • All URLs (host_permissions) — The content script must run on any web page to detect Japanese text and provide reading assistance.
  • Storage — To save your vocabulary bank, known words, and preferences.
  • Unlimited Storage — The bundled dictionary is roughly 50 MB and requires more than the default storage quota.
  • Active Tab — To communicate with the current tab for furigana and hover features.
  • Offscreen — To run the dictionary and tokenizer in a background document for performance, and to fetch and cache pronunciation clips when you press play.
  • Downloads — To save Anki export files and backup files to your Downloads folder.
  • Alarms — To schedule the automatic backup check. The check runs about once a day; a backup file is only written when the previous one is at least a week old and your data has actually changed, or once right after an update, so in practice you get at most one file per week and one per update.

Data Retention and Deletion

All stored data remains under your control:

  • Individual words can be removed from the vocabulary bank, and known words can be un-marked at any time.
  • Backup and Anki export files are ordinary files in your Downloads folder; delete them like any other file. This includes the weekly automatic backup described above, which contains your saved words together with their source sentences and page URLs.
  • Uninstalling the extension removes everything it stored inside the browser. It does not remove files already written to your Downloads folder — delete those yourself if you want them gone. Data in Chrome's sync area is managed by Chrome and your Google account, and may be restored if you reinstall the extension while Chrome Sync is active.
